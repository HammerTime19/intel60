How Intel60 works

Every 30 minutes, a short automated run turns new security news into 60-word briefs, publishes a page for every story and vulnerability, and alerts readers about the stories they care about. It takes a minute or two, with no servers to run.

FREE costs nothing PAID the only step that costs money (about $2–3 a month)
1

⏰ Timer goes off FREE

cron-job.org tells GitHub to start a run every 30 minutes. GitHub's own schedule runs too, as a backup.

2

⚙️ GitHub Actions starts a run FREE

GitHub lends a temporary cloud computer for a minute or two. It runs the pipeline, saves the results and publishes the site.

3

📰 Read the news FREE

Downloads the latest headlines from 8 RSS feeds, plus CISA's list of actively exploited vulnerabilities. About 150 items per run.

BleepingComputerThe Hacker NewsSecurityWeek Krebs on SecurityDark ReadingThe Record CyberScoopSANS ISCCISA KEV
4

🧹 Filter out what's not new FREE

Drops stories it has already processed, stories older than 48 hours, and ads, webinars and promos.

Usually only 0–5 new stories are left. If there are none, no AI runs and the run costs $0.
5

🤖 Claude writes the briefs PAID

New stories go to Claude Haiku (Anthropic) in batches of up to 8. For each story it returns:

Headline (≤10 words)60-word summary Severity: critical / high / infoCategory CVE IDsDuplicate check
When several outlets cover the same incident, they're merged into one card. Once 3 or more outlets cover it, the story is marked 🔥 trending. Each story is summarized once for everyone, so more readers don't cost more.
6

🔍 Add vulnerability facts FREE

For stories that mention CVEs, it looks up official public data, not AI guesses. New CVEs are checked again every 6 hours, because scores often arrive late.

What it is (NVD description)CVSS score (NVD) Exploit chance (FIRST EPSS)"Exploited" tag and patch deadline (CISA KEV)
7

📄 Build a page for every story and CVE FREE

Each story gets its own page, and each CVE gets a page combining its description, scores, exploitation status and every story that mentions it. A sitemap tells Google about all of them.

intel60.app/cve/intel60.app/story/…sitemap.xml
Pages stay up after a story leaves the 2-week feed, so links from search engines and shares never break.
8

💾 Save and publish FREE

cards.json holds all current stories, the app's whole database. state.json is the pipeline's memory: what it has seen, feed health and spending. Both are saved to GitHub, and GitHub Pages republishes the site.

If GitHub has a hiccup, saving is retried automatically. If publishing fails, the next run publishes again, so the site is never more than about 30 minutes behind.

Once the site is live, three things happen:

🌐 The site updates

Readers see the new briefs on their phone or laptop. Story and CVE pages appear in search engines and show their own headline when shared.

Everyone

🔔 Alerts on your device

Each new story is matched to every reader's choices, then sent as a notification. Sent only after publishing, so a tap always opens a live story.

Readers who turned on alerts

🔧 Owner reports

Repeated failed runs, AI errors, feeds that go down, the Monday spending report and a budget alert over $8.

Only the owner

🔔 How alerts work

  • Choose what you want: critical stories, newly exploited vulnerabilities, trending stories (3+ outlets) or stories about your watchlist vendors
  • Choose when: as they happen, or one summary each morning around 7 AM Eastern
  • Several matches at once arrive as one bundled notification, and the same story never alerts you twice
  • Delivered by your browser's own push service (Apple, Google or Mozilla): no email, phone number or account
  • Only an anonymous code for your device and your choices are stored, on a small Cloudflare Worker. Turning alerts off deletes them
  • On iPhone, add Intel60 to your Home Screen first, then turn alerts on from there

📱 Inside the app

  • On phones: swipe up for the next story; on laptops: a full website
  • Pick a day, or filter by severity, topic or trending
  • Search by vendor, CVE or keyword
  • My watchlist: only the vendors you run, saved on your device
  • Deadlines: countdown to patch every exploited bug
  • Trends: the week's busiest days and most-hit vendors
  • Tap a CVE for its own page; tap a dotted term for a plain-English explanation
  • Light and dark themes, works offline and updates itself

🛟 When something goes wrong

  • An AI reply can't be read: retried, then tried again next run
  • A feed is down: the others keep working
  • GitHub has an outage: saves retry, and missed publishes catch up next run
  • A device uninstalls or blocks alerts: its subscription is removed automatically
  • Two runs in a row fail: the owner is alerted, then told when it recovers
  • No connection: the app shows the last saved stories and says so
The key idea: the expensive work (Claude) happens once per story on GitHub's computer. Readers download the finished result, and alerts are matched and sent for free, which is why 1 reader and 1,000 readers cost the same.
